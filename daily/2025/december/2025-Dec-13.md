## Welsh notes

### Oscillators

The most fundamental sound is the sine wave. Every sound whether it is natural or
synthetic is made up of sine waves.

----

Harmonic amplitudes are important! The square and triangle waves both consist of only odd harmonics but have different shape because the rate at which the harmonic amplitudes decrease is different - faster for the triangle wave.

----

Additive synthesis is the process of creating complex waveforms by adding together simpler waveforms, usually sine waves, at different frequencies and amplitudes. Subtractive synthesis is the process of creating complex waveforms by starting with a harmonically rich waveform and then filtering out (subtracting) certain frequencies to shape the sound.

----

Sounds created by synthesizers are referred to as patches.

----

A waveform's usefulness lies with the harmonics that make it up and is NOT inherent to the shape of the waveform. Two sounds can have very similar harmonics yet have waveforms that look nothing alike. Adding waveforms together in an attempt to match the waveshape of an acoustic instrument won't produce desirable results most of the time.

----

While marimba sound is literally the sum of two triangle waves, to the ear it sounds far richer than the sum of its parts.

----

As a pulse becomes narrower it goes from a square wave with only odd partials to having both even and odd partials and therefore sounding more like a sawtooth wave.

----

Noise when used along with the filter, and the filter's resonance and envelope is far more useful than it may at first seem.

----
Ring modulation works by multiplying two signals together which produces an output that consists of frequencies that are the sum and difference of all the original harmonic frequencies.

The important point to consider is that in practical usage everY harmonic in one signal gets multiplied with every harmonic in the other. If the two signal s have tunings where man y of their harmonics coincide then the end result will be a harmonic sound with rich overtones.

### Filters

Synthesizers were born with the introduction of the filter. Of all the components on a synthesizer it is the most responsible for shaping the sound. Filters are used to change the timbre or tone color of these basic waveforms.

----

A common misconception with filters is that all frequencies beyond the cutoff frequency are removed from the signal. The filtering beyond the cutoff frequency actually takes place gradually. The rate or degree of filtering that takes place beyond the cutoff frequency is determined by the slope of the filter.

----

One pole is 6db per octave

----

Resonance acts as an amplifier at the cutoff when used with a low-pass or a high-pass filter. When used with a band-pass or band-reject filter it sets the width of the band.

### Envelopes

Whenever frequencies are removed from a signal they no longer contribute to the overall power of the signal. Removing frequencies will result in an overall lower amplitude.

----

Come in two flavors:

- Amplitude envelopes - control the overall volume of a sound over time, usually using the ADSR model (Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release). A, D and R are time durations, while S is a level - usually a percentage.

- Filter envelopes - control the cutoff frequency of a filter over time, also often using the ADSR model. Here, A, D and R are time durations, while S is a frequency level.

Filter envelope is an ADSR that controls the filter's cutoff frequency over time using the formula: cutoff = base + (envelope × amount). Example: base cutoff500 Hz, envelope amount 7500 Hz, ADSR (A:0.2s,D:0.5s, S:60%, R:1.0s). When key pressed, cutoffrises from 500 Hz (dull) to 8000 Hz (bright) duringattack, decays to 5000 Hz during decay phase,sustains at 5000 Hz while held, then falls back to500 Hz (dull) during release.### LFOs

### Low-Frequency Oscillators (LFOs)

Live in the inaudible frequency, usually below 20Hz. LFOs are used to modulate parameters of other modules, such as pitch (vibrato), amplitude (tremolo), or filter cutoff frequency (wah-wah effect).

Can play nice with PWM (pulse-width modulation) to create richer timbres.
