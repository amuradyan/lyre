# String harmonics
<!-- slide-id: 268e2073-47a6-41d3-9a17-abfedd75eba2 -->
<!-- tags: harmonics, physics, waveforms -->

When you pluck a string on a lyre, you don't just hear one frequency. The string vibrates in multiple modes simultaneously - the full length vibrates at the fundamental frequency, but half the string also vibrates at twice that frequency, a third at three times, and so on.

Each mode produces a harmonic - a frequency that's an integer multiple of the fundamental. The first harmonic is at 2×f with half the amplitude, the second at 3×f with a third the amplitude, the third at 4×f with a quarter the amplitude. This pattern continues, with each harmonic getting progressively quieter.

![harmonic-builder]()

This is why plucked strings sound bright and rich rather than pure like a tuning fork. A tuning fork produces nearly just the fundamental. A lyre string produces the fundamental plus all these overtones blended together.

Notice how as you add more harmonics, the waveform starts to look less like a smooth sine wave and more like a sawtooth - sharp rises followed by gradual falls. This sawtooth shape is characteristic of plucked and bowed string instruments.
